How Market Data Works

Our market data is sourced from the CoinGecko public API, which aggregates prices from hundreds of exchanges worldwide. We fetch real-time prices, 24-hour percentage changes, market capitalization, and 7-day sparkline data for over 100 cryptocurrencies. Data refreshes every 45 seconds.

Understanding the metrics:

  • Price: Current trading price in USD, updated every 45 seconds from CoinGecko.
  • 24h Change: Percentage price change over the past 24 hours. Green indicates a price increase, red indicates a decrease.
  • Market Cap: Total market value of all circulating tokens (price × circulating supply).

Understanding Cryptocurrency Markets

Cryptocurrency markets operate 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, across hundreds of exchanges worldwide. Unlike traditional stock markets, there is no central exchange or closing bell — prices move continuously based on supply, demand, news, and sentiment. This page tracks the top 100 cryptocurrencies by market capitalization, giving you a real-time snapshot of the most significant digital assets.

How to Read the Market Data

  • Price — The current trading price in USD, updated every 45 seconds from CoinGecko.
  • 24h Change — The percentage price change over the last 24 hours. Green indicates a price increase; red indicates a decrease.
  • Sparkline — A 7-day mini price chart showing the recent trend at a glance. An upward slope means the price has been rising over the week.
  • Market Cap — The total value of all coins in circulation (price × circulating supply). This is the primary metric for ranking cryptocurrencies.

What Drives Crypto Prices?

Cryptocurrency prices are influenced by a combination of factors:

  • Supply and demand — Bitcoin has a fixed supply cap of 21 million coins. When demand exceeds available supply, prices rise.
  • News and events — Regulatory announcements, exchange hacks, institutional adoption, and macroeconomic events can cause rapid price movements.
  • Market sentiment — Fear and greed cycles drive short-term price action. The Fear & Greed Index on our homepage tracks this daily.
  • Technology developments — Network upgrades, protocol changes, and new use cases can affect long-term valuations.
  • Liquidity — Higher trading volume and liquidity generally lead to more stable prices.

Major Cryptocurrencies

Bitcoin (BTC) — The first and largest cryptocurrency by market cap. Often called "digital gold," Bitcoin is designed as a store of value and medium of exchange with a fixed supply schedule.

Ethereum (ETH) — The second-largest cryptocurrency and the leading smart contract platform. Ethereum enables decentralized applications (dApps), DeFi protocols, and NFTs.

Solana (SOL) — A high-performance blockchain known for fast transaction speeds and low fees. Popular for DeFi, NFTs, and memecoin trading.

Stablecoins (USDT, USDC) — Cryptocurrencies pegged to the US dollar, providing stability in volatile markets. Widely used for trading, remittances, and as a store of value.
