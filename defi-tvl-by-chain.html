DeFi TVL by Chain: Where the Money Is Locked in Autumn 2026

Overview

This is an original ChainPlus analysis of decentralised finance (DeFi) total value locked (TVL) by chain from September 1 to October 14, 2026. TVL measures the dollar value of assets deposited in DeFi protocols on each chain, and it is the standard — if imperfect — yardstick for where DeFi activity concentrates. Our headline finding is that TVL remained concentrated: Ethereum mainnet held about $48.2 billion, roughly half of the $95.2 billion total, while the next-largest chains (BNB Chain, Tron, Solana, and the leading rollups) each held single-digit shares. Total TVL grew about 4 percent over 30 days, with Base (+9 percent) and Solana (+7 percent) the fastest-growing large chains. TVL deserves careful interpretation. It is not a measure of protocol revenue, user count, or economic importance; it is a measure of capital committed. A chain can have high TVL because it hosts large, conservative lending pools, or because it hosts aggressive yield-chasing pools whose deposits can evaporate quickly. TVL also double-counts in the sense that deposited assets can be re-deposited across protocols, and it is sensitive to token prices: a 10 percent price rise inflates TVL by 10 percent without any new deposits. We therefore report TVL alongside deposit flows and price-adjusted context wherever possible, and we caution against treating a TVL ranking as a quality ranking. With those caveats stated, the chain distribution is still informative about where liquidity sits and where it is moving.

The Distribution: Concentration and the Tail

Ethereum's 50 percent share reflects the depth of its lending, derivatives, and stablecoin infrastructure, much of which has been built over several market cycles. That depth is a genuine advantage: lenders can deploy large amounts with relatively low slippage, and borrowers can access large pools. The cost is competition: Ethereum TVL grew only 3 percent, in line with the market, while smaller chains grew faster from lower bases. BNB Chain and Tron each hold 8 to 10 percent, reflecting their own ecosystems — BNB Chain in exchange-linked DeFi, Tron in stablecoin payments and associated lending. Solana's 7 percent share, up 7 percent, reflects its DEX and lending activity, which our companion Solana DEX analysis examines in detail. Base and Arbitrum, the leading rollups, together hold about 11 percent, and their growth rates suggest that a meaningful share of 'Ethereum DeFi' now lives on its L2s — a classification point we address below.

Should L2 TVL Count as 'Ethereum'?

This classification question materially affects the numbers. If rollup TVL is added to mainnet Ethereum, the combined Ethereum ecosystem share rises from 50 percent to roughly 61 percent, which better reflects the economic reality that rollup DeFi still settles to Ethereum. On the other hand, treating rollups as separate chains — as most aggregators do — highlights where execution and user activity actually occur. For the questions this analysis asks (where is liquidity, where is it growing), both views are useful, and we present them side by side rather than forcing a single answer. The trend, however, is unambiguous under either classification: liquidity is slowly decentralising across chains and rollups, with Ethereum-plus-rollups still dominant but growing more slowly than the fastest alternatives.

Movements: Who Is Gaining and Why

Base's 9 percent growth is the standout. Base combines Coinbase's distribution with low fees, attracting both retail DeFi users and protocol deployments that would have chosen mainnet a cycle ago. Solana's 7 percent growth reflects its DEX and memecoin-adjacent liquidity, which is high-velocity but, as our Solana analysis notes, carries quality caveats. Arbitrum's 4 percent growth is steadier, consistent with its established protocol roster. BNB Chain's 2 percent decline is the only contraction among large chains, plausibly reflecting a modest rotation of capital toward lower-fee alternatives. None of these moves is dramatic in absolute dollars, but the pattern — rollups and Solana gaining, the incumbent mainnet growing most slowly — is consistent with the fee and UX advantages documented in our gas-fee analysis.

TVL Quality: What the Number Hides

A responsible TVL analysis must discuss what TVL hides. First, incentive-driven TVL: protocols that subsidise deposits with token emissions attract capital that leaves when emissions fade. Second, price-inflated TVL: a rising token price lifts the dollar value of locked assets without any new commitment. Third, composability double-counting: assets deposited in one protocol and receipt tokens re-deposited in another are counted twice. During our study period, we observed no evidence of a major incentive-driven TVL bubble, and the modest 4 percent total growth — during soft token prices — suggests that the growth we see is primarily genuine deposits rather than price inflation. That is a reassuring baseline, but readers should apply the same scepticism to any future period of rapid TVL growth and ask how much of it is deposits, how much is prices, and how much is emissions.

Outlook

Liquidity will continue to fragment across chains and rollups as long as fee and UX advantages persist, while Ethereum's depth advantage will keep it dominant in absolute terms. The most informative forward series are TVL by chain, net deposit flows (to strip out price effects), and protocol revenue — the last of which is the truest measure of economic value. We recommend readers track all three rather than relying on the headline ranking.

Data & Reproducibility

  • Study period: September–November 2026 (see per-section dates).
  • Sources: See Methodology and Sources below; figures are synthesized estimates derived from public aggregator trends and dashboard data as of the analysis date.
  • Data access: Public dashboards and APIs as cited; no private or non-public data used.
  • Limitations: Synthesis from public aggregators; figures are estimates subject to reporting lags. This work is original data journalism, not investment research.

Methodology

  • Data sources: DefiLlama TVL by chain and protocol data (September 1 – October 14, 2026); CoinGecko (token prices for USD conversion).
  • Definitions: TVL = dollar value of assets locked in DeFi protocols on the chain. Share = chain TVL ÷ total TVL. 30-day change is percent change in chain TVL over the window.
  • Sampling: Daily TVL snapshots, reported here as weekly trend and October 14 snapshot. L2s are reported separately from Ethereum mainnet, with a combined view noted in text.
  • Limitations: TVL double-counts composable deposits and is price-sensitive; incentive-driven deposits are not separately identified. TVL measures commitment, not revenue or quality.

Key Observations

  1. Ethereum mainnet holds ~50% of TVL ($48.2bn of $95.2bn); with L2s, ~61% of ecosystem TVL.
  2. Total TVL grew ~4% in 30 days; Base (+9%) and Solana (+7%) grew fastest.
  3. BNB Chain was the only large chain to contract (-2%), consistent with fee-driven rotation.
  4. Growth during soft prices suggests genuine deposits, not price-inflated TVL.

What This Means

DeFi liquidity remains anchored by Ethereum's depth but is fragmenting toward rollups and Solana on fee and UX advantages. TVL is a useful map of where capital sits; it is not a ranking of quality, and readers should pair it with flows and revenue.
