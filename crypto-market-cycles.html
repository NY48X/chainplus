Understanding Crypto Market Cycles

By ChainPlus Editorial · October 28, 2026 · Analysis

Cryptocurrency markets move in cycles. Understanding these cycles can help you make better decisions about when to buy, sell, or hold. Here's what you need to know about crypto market cycles.

The Four Phases

1. Accumulation

After a bear market, prices stabilize at low levels. Smart money and long-term investors begin buying. Volume is low, sentiment is negative, and most retail participants have left the market. This phase can last months or years.

2. Markup (Bull Market)

Prices begin rising. Early adopters and institutional investors enter. Media coverage increases. Retail FOMO (fear of missing out) kicks in. Prices rise rapidly, often exceeding previous all-time highs. This phase can last 1-2 years.

3. Distribution

Prices peak and begin to plateau. Smart money starts selling. Retail investors are still buying, thinking the bull market will continue. Volume remains high but prices stop rising. This is the most dangerous phase — it's hard to tell if it's a pause or the top.

4. Markdown (Bear Market)

Prices decline. Panic selling sets in. Retail investors exit. Projects fail. The media declares crypto "dead." This phase can be brutal — Bitcoin has dropped 70-80% from its peak in previous bear markets. But it also sets the stage for the next accumulation phase.

What Drives Cycles?

  • Halving events: Bitcoin's supply reduction every ~4 years is a known catalyst.
  • Macro conditions: Interest rates, liquidity, and risk appetite affect crypto.
  • Regulatory developments: ETF approvals, enforcement actions, and new rules.
  • Technological progress: Network upgrades, new use cases, and adoption.
  • Sentiment: Fear and greed cycles drive short-term price action.

How to Use This Knowledge

Understanding market cycles doesn't mean you can time the market perfectly. But it can help you:

  • Avoid buying at the top: If everyone is talking about crypto, it might be near a peak.
  • Avoid selling at the bottom: If the media is declaring crypto dead, it might be near a bottom.
  • Adjust position sizing: Be more conservative in late-stage bull markets, more aggressive in accumulation phases.
  • Stay patient: Cycles take years. Don't expect to get rich in a month.

The Reality

Market cycles are descriptive, not predictive. They tell you what happened, not what will happen. Each cycle has unique characteristics driven by different factors. The best approach is to understand the cycle, stick to your strategy, and avoid emotional decisions.

Historical Examples

2017 Bull Market: Bitcoin went from $1,000 to $20,000. ICOs raised billions. The crash in 2018 wiped out 80%+ of value. Many projects died.

2020-2021 Bull Market: Bitcoin went from $10,000 to $69,000. DeFi summer brought billions in TVL. NFTs exploded. The 2022 crash (triggered by Terra/LUNA and FTX) wiped out 70%+ of value.

2023-2024 Recovery: Bitcoin ETF approval drove institutional interest. Prices recovered gradually. New narratives (AI, real-world assets, restaking) emerged.

Common Mistakes

  • Buying at the top: FOMO drives people to buy when prices are highest. This is the worst time to enter.
  • Selling at the bottom: Panic drives people to sell when prices are lowest. This locks in losses.
  • Ignoring macro: Crypto doesn't exist in a vacuum. Interest rates, liquidity, and risk appetite matter.
  • Over-leveraging: Using leverage in volatile markets is a recipe for liquidation.

Bitcoin Dominance

Bitcoin dominance (BTC's share of total crypto market cap) is a useful cycle indicator. In bull markets, altcoins often outperform Bitcoin, causing BTC dominance to drop. In bear markets, capital flows back to Bitcoin, causing dominance to rise.

High BTC dominance (>50%) often indicates a bear market or early recovery. Low dominance (<40%) often indicates a late-stage bull market with altcoin speculation.

Altcoin Seasons

An "altcoin season" occurs when altcoins significantly outperform Bitcoin. This typically happens in the middle to late stages of a bull market, when risk appetite is high and capital flows from Bitcoin to smaller, riskier assets.

Altcoin seasons are often followed by severe corrections. The assets that rose the most in the bull market often fall the most in the bear market. This is why diversification and position sizing are important.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you time the crypto market?

No one can consistently time the market. Market cycles are descriptive, not predictive. The best approach is dollar-cost averaging (DCA) — investing a fixed amount regularly regardless of price. This removes emotion and timing from the equation.

How long do crypto cycles last?

Bitcoin's halving cycle is approximately 4 years. Bull markets typically last 1-2 years, bear markets 1-2 years, with accumulation phases in between. But each cycle is unique and timing is unpredictable.

Should I buy during a bear market?

Bear markets offer the best entry points for long-term investors. Prices are lower and risk is reduced. But catching the bottom is impossible — prices can always go lower. DCA throughout the bear market is a safer approach than trying to time the bottom.

What's the best strategy for crypto investing?

For most people: DCA into Bitcoin and Ethereum over a long time horizon. Don't try to time the market. Don't use leverage. Don't invest more than you can afford to lose. Rebalance periodically. Stay informed but avoid emotional reactions to price movements.
