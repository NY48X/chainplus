Overview

This is an original ChainPlus analysis of Solana decentralised exchange (DEX) volumes from September 1 to October 20, 2026. We compare the leading venues — Raydium, the Jupiter aggregator, Orca, and Meteora — using weekly volume in US dollars and market-share estimates drawn from public aggregators. The headline finding is that Solana DEX volume remained robust at roughly $48.5 billion per week, with Raydium holding the largest share at about 38 percent, Jupiter routing about 29 percent, and a competitive fringe of concentrated-liquidity venues (Orca and Meteora) together accounting for roughly a quarter of flow. Volume grew about 5 percent over the 30-day window, led by Jupiter's routing gains. Understanding the venue split requires understanding the roles. Raydium and Orca are liquidity venues: pools of token pairs live on their contracts and traders swap against them. Jupiter is an aggregator: it finds the best price across venues and routes orders accordingly, so its 'volume' overlaps conceptually with the venues it routes through. Meteora specialises in dynamic and concentrated liquidity for volatile pairs. Comparing aggregator volume directly against venue volume is therefore apples-to-oranges unless the methodology is stated clearly, which is why our methodology section distinguishes routed volume from venue volume and why readers should watch both series rather than treating the ranking as a simple league table. Solana's DEX activity also reflects broader market conditions. Memecoin and token-launch trading, which spike on Solana, can inflate volumes rapidly, while quieter weeks compress them. Our study period was relatively typical rather than euphoric, which makes it a useful baseline: the venue shares we observe are more likely to reflect structural preferences (liquidity depth, fees, routing quality) than transient speculation. That baseline matters for the outlook question of whether the ranking is stable or fragile.

Venue Share and the Aggregator Effect

Raydium's lead is consistent with its deep liquidity in the most traded pairs, particularly against SOL and major stablecoins. Jupiter's growth is the more interesting movement: up 11 percent over 30 days, the fastest of the large venues. That growth is best read as a routing preference rather than a liquidity advantage — traders increasingly let Jupiter choose the venue per swap, and the aggregator captures the flow even when the underlying pool belongs to a rival. Orca, by contrast, slipped 3 percent, plausibly because some of its flow migrated to Jupiter-routed orders against the same pools. Meteora's 4 percent gain suggests its specialised pools for volatile assets continued to attract flow during a period of active long-tail trading. The practical implication is that 'volume share' on Solana is increasingly a story about order-flow routing rather than pool ownership. A venue can lose share not because its pools worsened but because the order flow that used to arrive directly now passes through an aggregator first. For protocol designers, this shifts the competitive question from 'how deep are my pools' to 'how reliably do aggregators route to my pools'. For traders and researchers, it means that headline volume rankings should always be footnoted with the aggregator overlap, as we do in the table below.

Liquidity Depth and Fee Economics

Volume alone does not tell the whole story; the cost of trading does. Concentrated-liquidity venues such as Orca and Meteora offer lower fees and tighter spreads on their covered pairs because liquidity providers can concentrate capital in narrow price ranges. The trade-off is impermanent-loss risk when prices leave the range, which is why such pools suit stable or correlated pairs. Raydium's broader pools serve the long tail of pairs where concentration is less practical. Jupiter, as an aggregator, earns no pool fees but captures routing value and, increasingly, its own swap fees depending on the integration. The net effect for users is that the cheapest route depends on the pair and the size of the trade, which is exactly the calculation aggregators automate. We estimate that the fee-weighted cost of a $10,000 swap on the major pairs fell slightly over the period as fee tiers compressed and routing competition intensified. That compression is a direct consequence of aggregator competition: when Jupiter can compare venues in real time, venues must keep fees competitive to win routed flow. The beneficiaries are traders; the pressure falls on liquidity providers, whose returns depend on maintaining competitive fees without over-concentrating capital.

Is the Volume Real?

A reasonable scepticism applies to all DEX volume figures, because wash trading and incentive-driven volume can inflate them. Solana is not immune. We apply two sanity checks. First, we cross-reference reported volume against on-chain transaction counts and pool inflows; venues where volume rises while transactions and liquidity fall are flagged as lower quality. Second, we compare spot DEX volume against centralised exchange volumes for the same pairs; a persistent, implausible DEX premium would suggest inflation. During our study period, none of the top venues failed these checks egregiously, though the long-tail venues trailing the leaders showed more variance and should be read with wider error bars. Our confidence is therefore highest in the aggregate ($48.5 billion per week) and in Raydium's and Jupiter's leading positions, and more tentative in the precise ordering of the smaller venues. Readers should also note that volume is not value. A venue can process large volumes with thin margins, while a smaller venue earns more per dollar traded if its fees are higher or its order flow is more valuable. Without fee-revenue data, which venues do not publish consistently, we cannot rank by profitability, only by flow. We state this limitation plainly rather than implying that the largest venue is the most successful business.

Outlook

The coming quarter will test whether Jupiter's routing growth continues to compress venue share or whether Raydium and others rebalance by offering exclusives or fee advantages to aggregators. Memecoin cycles will also move the aggregate up or down sharply. For observers, the most informative dashboard remains three series: total Solana DEX volume, the aggregator-routed share, and fee revenue per venue where available. For traders, the practical advice is unchanged and boring in the best way: use an aggregator for price discovery, check slippage settings carefully on volatile pairs, and diversify wallet exposure given Solana's history of bridge and wallet incidents (see our wallet-security guide).

Data & Reproducibility

  • Study period: September–November 2026 (see per-section dates).
  • Sources: See Methodology and Sources below; all cited sources were accessed during the study period.
  • Data access: Public dashboards and APIs as cited; no private or non-public data used.
  • Limitations: Synthesis from public aggregators; figures are estimates subject to reporting lags. This work is original data journalism, not investment research.

Methodology

  • Data sources: DefiLlama DEX volume aggregator (venue volumes, September 1 – October 20, 2026); Jupiter swap data for routed volumes; Solana Beach for transaction counts.
  • Definitions: Weekly volume in USD, venue share = venue volume ÷ total Solana DEX volume. Aggregator volumes are shown separately because they overlap venue pools.
  • Sanity checks: Cross-referencing volume against on-chain transactions and stablecoin inflows; comparison against centralised exchange volumes for major pairs.
  • Limitations: Wash trading cannot be fully excluded; fee-revenue data are incomplete, so rankings are by flow, not profitability. Incentive-driven volume before the study window may affect baselines.

Key Observations

  1. Raydium leads on venue volume at $18.4bn/week (38%); Jupiter routes $14.2bn/week (29%) and grew fastest (+11%).
  2. Total Solana DEX volume held near $48.5bn/week, up about 5% over 30 days.
  3. Aggregator growth compresses venue fees and shifts competition from pool depth to routing quality.
  4. Volume quality checks passed for top venues; smaller venues carry wider uncertainty.

What This Means

Solana's DEX ecosystem remains liquid and competitive, with routing — not just pools — deciding share. Traders benefit from aggregator competition in the form of tighter prices and lower effective fees. Observers should track routed share and fee revenue, not just headline volume, to judge whether any venue's lead is durable.
