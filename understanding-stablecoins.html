Understanding Stablecoins: USDT, USDC, and Beyond

By Mike Tembo · September 5, 2026 · Explainer

Stablecoins are cryptocurrencies designed to maintain a stable value, typically pegged to the US dollar. They serve as the bridge between traditional finance and crypto, providing stability in volatile markets.

Why Stablecoins Matter

Crypto markets are volatile. Bitcoin can drop 10% in a day. Stablecoins solve this by maintaining a fixed value — 1 USDT = $1, 1 USDC = $1. This makes them useful for:

  • Trading: Move in and out of volatile assets without converting to fiat.
  • Remittances: Send money across borders instantly and cheaply.
  • DeFi: Provide liquidity, earn yield, and collateralize loans.
  • Savings: Hold dollar-equivalent value in crypto wallets.

Types of Stablecoins

Fiat-Backed (USDT, USDC)

These are backed by reserves of fiat currency and short-term government securities. For every token in circulation, the issuer holds $1 in reserves. Tether (USDT) and Circle (USDC) are the largest fiat-backed stablecoins.

Risk: Reserve transparency. Tether has historically been opaque about its reserves. Circle is more transparent but still relies on traditional banking infrastructure.

Crypto-Backed (DAI)

DAI is backed by crypto collateral (primarily Ethereum). Users deposit crypto into smart contracts and mint DAI against it. The collateral ratio is typically 150%+ — you need to deposit $150 of ETH to mint $100 of DAI.

Risk: Collateral volatility. If the collateral drops in value, the position can be liquidated. This happened during the March 2020 crash.

Algorithmic (UST - collapsed)

Algorithmic stablecoins maintain their peg through code, not reserves. Terra's UST used a burn/mint mechanism with its sister token LUNA. When confidence broke, the mechanism failed catastrophically — UST depegged to near zero and LUNA collapsed.

Lesson: Algorithmic stablecoins are extremely risky. The UST collapse wiped out $40+ billion in value and is the largest failure in crypto history.

Choosing a Stablecoin

For most users, USDC is the safest choice due to its transparency and regulatory compliance. USDT has the highest liquidity and is widely accepted on exchanges. DAI is the most decentralized option but has more complex risks.

Avoid algorithmic stablecoins unless you fully understand the mechanism and accept the risk of total loss.

Stablecoins in DeFi

Stablecoins are the backbone of DeFi. Most lending protocols, DEXs, and yield farms use stablecoins as their primary unit of account. Without stablecoins, DeFi would be impractical — no one wants to take out a loan denominated in a volatile asset.

USDC and DAI are the most used stablecoins in DeFi. USDC is preferred for its stability and transparency. DAI is preferred by those who want a decentralized option. USDT is widely used but less trusted in DeFi due to transparency concerns.

Regulatory Outlook

Stablecoin regulation is coming. The EU's MiCA framework requires stablecoin issuers to hold reserves in approved assets and undergo regular audits. The US is considering similar legislation. This will likely benefit established players (Circle, Tether) who can afford compliance, while making it harder for new entrants.

The key question is whether regulation will foster innovation or protect incumbents. If regulation is too strict, it could drive stablecoin activity to less regulated jurisdictions. If too loose, it could allow another Terra-style collapse.

Stablecoin Risks in Detail

Beyond the type-specific risks, all stablecoins share common vulnerabilities:

  • Depegging: Any stablecoin can lose its peg if confidence breaks. Even fiat-backed stablecoins have depegged temporarily during market stress.
  • Regulatory risk: Governments could restrict or ban stablecoins. This would affect their usability and value.
  • Counterparty risk: Fiat-backed stablecoins depend on the issuer's honesty and the banking system's stability.
  • Smart contract risk: Crypto-backed and algorithmic stablecoins depend on smart contracts that can have bugs.

The Future of Stablecoins

Stablecoins are likely to become more regulated and more integrated with traditional finance. Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) may compete with or complement private stablecoins. The key question is whether stablecoins will remain a crypto-native innovation or become absorbed into the traditional financial system.

For users, the practical advice is: diversify across stablecoin types, monitor reserve transparency, and stay informed about regulatory developments. Stablecoins are useful tools but not risk-free.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which stablecoin is the safest?

USDC is generally considered the safest due to its transparency, regulatory compliance, and reserve composition (primarily cash and short-term Treasuries). USDT has higher liquidity but less transparency. DAI is the most decentralized but has more complex risks.

Can stablecoins lose their peg?

Yes. Even fiat-backed stablecoins have temporarily depegged during market stress. In 2023, USDC depegged to $0.87 when Silicon Valley Bank (where Circle held reserves) failed. It recovered within days, but the incident showed that stablecoins are not risk-free.

Are stablecoins a good investment?

Stablecoins are not investments — they're tools for stability. They don't appreciate in value. They're useful for trading, remittances, and DeFi, but holding stablecoins long-term means losing purchasing power to inflation.

What's the difference between USDT and USDC?

USDT (Tether) has higher liquidity and is more widely accepted on exchanges. USDC (Circle) is more transparent and regulatory compliant. USDT's reserves include a mix of assets; USDC's are primarily cash and Treasuries. For DeFi, USDC is generally preferred.
