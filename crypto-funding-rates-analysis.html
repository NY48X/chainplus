Overview

This is an original ChainPlus analysis of crypto perpetual futures funding rates from September 1 to October 28, 2026, covering Bitcoin and Ethereum perps on major exchanges and the leverage and sentiment dynamics they reveal. Funding rates are the periodic payments between long and short position holders in perpetual futures contracts, which pay no expiry but track spot prices; the funding rate is the mechanism that anchors the perpetual price to spot. When the rate is positive, longs pay shorts, indicating that longs are competing aggressively for exposure; when negative, shorts pay longs. Our headline finding is that funding rates were positive but moderating throughout the period: the annualised BTC funding rate fell from roughly 18.2 percent in early September to about 3.2 percent by late October, while the long/short ratio cooled from elevated levels to a near-balanced 1.07. Open interest remained substantial at about $21.3 billion across BTC and ETH perps. Funding rates are best understood as a sentiment and positioning thermometer, not as a price predictor. Persistently high positive funding can coincide with rising prices (genuine demand) or with crowded speculation that precedes liquidations (fragile demand), and the rate alone cannot distinguish the two. What it can do is measure how much longs are paying to hold their positions, which is a direct cost that must be justified by expected price appreciation. When that cost is high relative to plausible appreciation, positions are fragile. Our period's moderation — high funding in September giving way to neutral funding in October — therefore describes a market that became less crowded and less expensive to hold longs in, which is consistent with the soft spot prices and steady ETF accumulation we document in companion analyses.

Funding Levels and the Cost of Long Exposure

The decline from an annualised 18.2 percent to 3.2 percent is the central fact of the period. At 18.2 percent annualised, a long position cost roughly 0.05 percent every eight hours, or about $500 per $100,000 of exposure per day — expensive enough to force a tactical decision about whether the position was worth holding. At 3.2 percent, the same position cost roughly $90 per day per $100,000, a carry cost that is easily borne by a strategic holder. The transition between those regimes occurred gradually over eight weeks rather than in a single liquidation event, which suggests orderly deleveraging and positioning rather than a cascade. The absence of a cascade is itself information: it indicates that the market absorbed the softening spot prices without the forced-selling dynamics that characterise fragile leverage.

Long/Short Positioning and Open Interest

The long/short ratio tells a complementary story. In early September, longs dominated at about 1.25 to 1 based on our venue sampling; by late October the ratio had cooled to about 1.07, near balance. That cooling, combined with stable open interest, describes a market that retained its size while shedding its directional crowding — the hallmark of a maturing rather than a collapsing derivatives market. Open interest near $21.3 billion across BTC and ETH is large in absolute terms, but its stability during soft prices is more informative than its level: it indicates that derivatives traders continued to provide liquidity and hedging services even as spot softened. A falling open interest during soft prices would have suggested broader withdrawal; that did not occur.

Venue Comparison

Funding rates were broadly similar across Binance, Bybit, and OKX, with differences of a few tenths of a percent annualised reflecting each venue's order-book composition rather than structural divergence. That similarity is healthy: it indicates that arbitrage between venues kept funding aligned, which is what an efficient perpetual market should do. Persistent large divergences between venues would suggest fragmented liquidity or capital controls; none were observed during the period beyond the normal intraday variation. Readers who trade perps should nevertheless monitor venue-specific rates, because the cheapest venue for a given position can differ by enough to matter at scale, and because liquidation thresholds and insurance funds differ by venue — a reminder that funding rate is one dimension of venue choice, not the only one.

Liquidation Risk and What to Watch

Liquidation risk is a function of leverage, position size, and price movement. At neutral funding and balanced positioning, the risk of a cascade is lower than at crowded, high-funding extremes, but it is not zero: a sharp spot move can still trigger liquidations among the most leveraged positions regardless of the funding regime. The prudent interpretation of our period is therefore that the market entered late October in a healthier, less crowded state than it entered September, which lowers (but does not eliminate) the probability of a cascade. For traders, the actionable guidance is standard and bears repeating: size positions so that a plausible adverse move does not threaten solvency, monitor funding as a cost rather than a signal, and treat funding spikes as a prompt to review crowded exposure rather than as a directional bet.

Outlook

Funding rates will continue to oscillate with positioning, and the key forward variables are the long/short ratio, open interest, and the spot price path. A return to high positive funding alongside rising prices would suggest renewed crowding; sustained neutral funding alongside rising open interest would suggest healthy growth. The joint dashboard — funding, ratio, and open interest — is the right tool, and we will continue to publish it quarterly.

Methodology

  • Data sources: Coinglass funding-rate dashboard (annualised funding, open interest); Binance, Bybit, and OKX perpetual order-book data. September 1 – October 28, 2026.
  • Definitions: Funding rate annualised as (funding per 8-hour interval × 365 × 3). Long/short ratio = long accounts ÷ short accounts (venue-reported). Open interest in USD at spot prices.
  • Sampling: Daily funding snapshots averaged weekly; long/short ratios as venue-reported daily averages.
  • Limitations: Venue-reported ratios use different account definitions and are indicative, not directly comparable. Funding rates smooth intraday spikes. This analysis does not assess any venue's solvency or liquidation-engine quality.

Key Observations

  1. BTC funding fell from ~18.2% to ~3.2% annualised — crowded leverage gave way to neutral carry.
  2. Long/short ratio cooled from ~1.25 to ~1.07; open interest stayed high (~$21.3bn).
  3. Funding was aligned across Binance, Bybit, and OKX — efficient perp arbitrage.
  4. Gradual deleveraging without a cascade indicates orderly softening, not fragile leverage.

What This Means

Perpetual funding in autumn 2026 describes a market that deleveraged orderly from crowded longs to neutral carry. Funding is a positioning thermometer, not a price predictor, and its moderation is a sign of healthier — not necessarily bullish — derivatives markets.
