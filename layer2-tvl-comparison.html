Overview

This is an original ChainPlus comparison of Ethereum Layer 2 total value locked (TVL) from September 1 to October 29, 2026, covering Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, zkSync Era, and Linea. Total L2 TVL stood at roughly $17.5 billion in late October, with Base (31 percent) and Arbitrum (27 percent) as the clear leaders, Optimism at 17 percent, and the zero-knowledge (zk) rollups — zkSync and Linea — together at about 17 percent. Total L2 TVL grew about 4 percent over 30 days, with Base the fastest-growing major rollup at 9 percent. The study also tracks sequencer activity (transactions per second on each rollup) to distinguish TVL that reflects genuine usage from TVL that reflects passive deposits. The Layer 2 landscape in 2026 is defined by a convergence on optimistic rollups — Arbitrum and Optimism, which assume transactions are valid and allow a challenge window — and a smaller but architecturally distinct cohort of zk-rollups, which generate cryptographic validity proofs. Base is technically an Optimism-stack chain operated by Coinbase. That shared stack matters: Base and Optimism share significant infrastructure, which affects both their growth and their interoperability prospects. Readers should also understand that TVL on a rollup is ultimately secured by Ethereum, but the immediate custody and bridge risk depends on the rollup's contracts and governance — a distinction our methodology section makes explicit and our risk section returns to.

The Leaders: Base and Arbitrum

Base's lead is best explained by distribution. Coinbase's user base, built-in on-ramps, and retail-oriented ecosystem create a funnel that other rollups must replicate through their own partnerships. Its 9 percent growth rate is consistent with that funnel operating steadily rather than speculatively. Arbitrum, the longer-established rollup, holds a slightly smaller share but the deepest DeFi ecosystem among rollups, which explains its resilience: even as Base grows faster, Arbitrum's protocol depth retains liquidity that is costly to move. The two leaders together hold 58 percent of L2 TVL, a concentration that mirrors the mainnet DeFi market and suggests that rollup competition, like DeFi competition, rewards depth and distribution over novelty.

The Challenger Pack

Optimism, at 17 percent and slightly contracting, faces the most interesting strategic question. Its Superchain vision — a network of interoperable chains sharing infrastructure — is now substantially realised in Base, which is itself an Optimism-stack chain. The question is whether Optimism captures value as the neutral infra layer or whether each chain in the stack captures value independently. Its TVL dynamics during the period were flat to down, which suggests the market has not yet settled that question in Optimism's favour. zkSync and Linea, the leading zk-rollups, together hold about 17 percent and grew modestly. Their architectural promise — cryptographic finality rather than fraud-proof challenge windows — is real, but during our period it had not yet translated into TVL leadership. That gap between architectural merit and market share is the central analytical puzzle of the zk cohort, and it likely reflects the time required to build protocol depth comparable to the optimistic leaders, rather than any deficiency in the cryptography.

TVL versus Activity: Which Rollup Is Used?

TVL without activity is capital at rest; activity without TVL is churn. We therefore pair TVL with sequencer throughput. Base leads on transactions per second (about 2.1) as well as TVL, confirming that its deposits reflect usage. Arbitrum follows at about 1.8 transactions per second. The zk rollups show lower throughput relative to their TVL, which may reflect both smaller user bases and different batching parameters. The broader point is methodological: any comparison that ranks rollups by TVL alone will overstate the position of chains where deposits are passive and understate chains where activity is high-velocity. Risks: Bridges, Sequencers and Governance

Three structural risks apply across the L2 landscape. First, bridge risk: assets move between Ethereum and rollups through contracts whose correctness is critical; bridge exploits remain the most consequential failure mode in the ecosystem's history. Second, sequencer risk: each rollup currently operates a centralised sequencer that orders transactions, which is a liveness and censorship consideration even where ultimate security rests with Ethereum. Third, governance and upgrade risk: the ability of a rollup's contracts to be upgraded — often by a multisig or token-holder vote — can change security assumptions. None of these risks materialised for the major rollups during our study period, but they are the right lens for evaluating any rollup, and they are why our related reading includes DeFi TVL by chain and gas-fee analysis, where the same migration dynamics appear from different angles.

Outlook

The rollup market is consolidating around two leaders with a differentiated challenger pack. The coming quarter's key questions are whether Optimism's stack vision yields measurable value capture, whether zk-rollups close the TVL gap, and whether interoperability between rollups (rather than just with mainnet) advances. We will track TVL, throughput, and bridge volumes as the joint evidentiary base.

Data & Reproducibility

  • Study period: September–November 2026 (see per-section dates).
  • Sources: See Methodology and Sources below; all cited sources were accessed during the study period.
  • Data access: Public dashboards and APIs as cited; no private or non-public data used.
  • Limitations: Synthesis from public aggregators; figures are estimates subject to reporting lags. This work is original data journalism, not investment research.

Methodology

  • Data sources: L2Beat (L2 TVL, sequencer activity); DefiLlama L2 TVL trackers. September 1 – October 29, 2026.
  • Definitions: TVL in USD; share = rollup TVL ÷ total L2 TVL. Sequencer throughput is average transactions per second reported by the rollup and aggregated by L2Beat.
  • Sampling: Daily TVL snapshots; throughput as 7-day averages to smooth batching variance.
  • Limitations: TVL double-counts composable deposits and is price-sensitive; sequencer throughput depends on batching parameters. Bridge and sequencer risk assessments are qualitative and based on public documentation.

Key Observations

  1. Base leads L2 TVL ($5.4bn, 31%); Arbitrum second ($4.7bn, 27%); together 58%.
  2. Base grew fastest among majors (+9%); Optimism was flat to down (-1%).
  3. zk-rollups (zkSync, Linea) hold ~17% combined — architectural merit ahead of TVL leadership.
  4. Base leads on both TVL and throughput (~2.1 tx/s), confirming usage-backed deposits.

What This Means

The rollup race in 2026 is a distribution race: Base's Coinbase funnel and Arbitrum's DeFi depth lead, while Optimism's stack vision and zk-rollups' cryptographic finality remain promises whose value is not yet fully captured in TVL.
